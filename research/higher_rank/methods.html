Rank-five finite T-data

There are 55 indecomposable rank-five families with identity symmetrizer and diagonal leading matrix. The classification combines a finite enumeration of 4,697 parity-compatible constant pairs, unbounded polynomial-lift checks and exact mutation certificates. The arguments below explain the reductions used in the computation.

A finite search over coefficient sums

Write P = N+(1), M = N−(1), so A+(1) = 2I − P and A−(1) = 2I − M. The necessary positivity theorem gives a row vector v > 0 with v(2I − P) > 0 and v(2I − M) > 0. Indecomposability makes the union support graph strongly connected, by the lowest-degree crossing argument in the rank-four manuscript.

Every off-diagonal weight wᵢⱼ = max(Pᵢⱼ,Mᵢⱼ) obeys wᵢⱼvᵢ < 2vⱼ. Multiplying around a simple directed cycle bounds its product by 2r in rank r. Since its weights are positive integers, every coefficient sum is at most 2r − 1. Diagonal sums are zero or one.

Relabel so v₁ ≥ ··· ≥ vᵣ. Above the diagonal, each column of P or M is zero or has a single 1, with the latter forcing its diagonal entry to be zero. Thus each upper-triangular part has (r+1)! possibilities. The enumerator processes all unordered pairs, then solves the constant symplectic identity row by row using exact adjugates and divisibility tests. Positive principal minors make each solved leading block invertible.

Stronger bounds from the ordered positive vector

For a known upper arrow i → j, one has vᵢ < 2vⱼ. Moving from a larger index to a smaller one uses the inequality vᵢ ≤ vⱼ with no factor. Let d be the smallest number of upper arrows in a path from k to j, allowing those free backwards moves. Then vₖ ≤ 2dvⱼ, and hence every lower weight in position (j,k) satisfies

wⱼₖ < 2d+1,   wⱼₖ ≤ 2d+1 − 1.

These paths depend only on the fixed upper triangles and can be computed before enumerating lower rows. For example, an arrow from the first to the last species makes d = 1 for every lower position, reducing every lower bound to 3. Strong connectivity guarantees an upper arrow across each ordered cut, so these bounds are finite.

The unknown M row is an affine-linear function of the unknown P row. Bounds on its entries and positivity of the next leading determinant therefore give linear inequalities. Interval bounds on unassigned row entries discard only branches that cannot satisfy these necessary inequalities. The final candidate still passes the original integer cycle and principal-minor checks.

The parity lemma is also applied as soon as a row is complete: every diagonal entry of (2I − P)(2I − M)T is even. This follows because the corresponding reciprocal Laurent polynomial has constant coefficient 2 and its nonzero powers occur in equal pairs.

Finally, exact extreme rays test the ordered simultaneous-positive cone. Canonicalization exhausts index permutations and exchange of signs. The generalized enumerator reproduces exactly the 460 parity-compatible rank-four candidates obtained by the earlier implementation.

Polynomial lifts without a delay cutoff

Excluding a principal subdatum by the lower-rank classification

Let S be a set of species. Suppose that, for each column k outside S, either N+[S,k] = 0 or N−[S,k] = 0. Every exterior-column contribution to the symplectic identity restricted to rows S is then zero. Consequently the principal matrices A±[S,S] themselves form a T-datum.

Simultaneous positivity passes to this principal subdatum: deleting an exterior row removes a nonpositive contribution to each column product vA±. Split its union support into weakly connected components. Each component is therefore an indecomposable T-datum with simultaneous positivity.

In ranks at most four, the completed lower-rank lift analysis classifies all such polynomial data under simultaneous positivity, and every surviving lift family is periodic. Hence the constant pair of each component must occur among the corresponding lower-rank catalogue constants, up to permutation and sign exchange. A missing pair is an exact obstruction. The saved witness identifies S, the zero sign in every exterior column, and the offending component. This argument uses the lower-rank positivity classification; it does not assume that restriction preserves periodicity in general.

A monomial tree and one extra species

After possibly exchanging signs, suppose N+ vanishes on all rows except an extra species ℓ, and (N+)ℓℓ = 0. On the other species, let N− have symmetric binary coefficient sums, with its off-diagonal support a connected tree. Diagonal monomials are allowed. Suppose its only arrow from that tree to ℓ is a single monomial at a → ℓ.

The translated-binomial identities give a common tree delay R. Diagonal monomials have exponent R/2. Species shifts can center every tree edge at exponent R/2 because the graph is a tree. These shifts may produce Laurent monomials; all coefficients remain nonnegative, and opposite signs remain disjoint.

Write q = zR/2, F = 1 + q², and let C be the tree’s symmetric binary coefficient matrix, including any diagonal loops. Let Tᵢ = (N+)ℓᵢ(z−1) and Uᵢ = (N−)ℓᵢ(z−1) in these shifted coordinates. The attaching monomial gives H = zᵇ(1 + z−rℓ). The equations between the tree and ℓ are

F(T − U) − qCT = −eₐH.

At the smallest exponent among T, U and H, the terms multiplied by q or q² contribute nothing. Disjoint support therefore forces that exponent to be min deg H = min deg Uₐ, with the same nonzero coefficient. At the largest exponent after subtracting R from deg H, the q² terms give similarly max deg Uₐ = max deg H − R.

Thus Uₐ cannot vanish. If its coefficient sum is one, it is a monomial; the two extremal equalities give rℓ = R and FUₐ = H. Define U′ = U − eₐUₐ. The equation becomes F(T − U′) = qCT. Comparing the smallest exponent of any nonzero T or U′ forces equal coefficients in opposite signs, contrary to disjoint support. Hence T = U′ = 0.

This excludes a reverse coefficient (N−)ℓₐ(1) of zero. If that coefficient is one, it excludes every case with any N+ support from ℓ to the tree, or any further N− support from ℓ to a different tree vertex. The argument is independent of rank and of the numerical delays.

Each unit of a coefficient sum receives an exponent variable. Delays are positive; a row-i exponent satisfies 0 < p < rᵢ; opposite signs have disjoint support. The symplectic identity is equality of finite multisets of affine-linear Laurent exponents. The solver encodes equality of their multiplicities. Thus UNSAT excludes the constant pair at all delays.

Two redundant linear consequences are included to simplify these queries. If two rows of one sign N vanish and the opposite off-diagonal entries between them are monomials, reciprocity forces their delays to agree and the two exponents to sum to that delay. If each off-diagonal entry of a row uses only one sign and exactly one diagonal support monomial occurs, its exponent is half the row delay. Both assertions follow by comparing the least and greatest exponents of the corresponding reciprocal identity.

Both integer arithmetic and rational arithmetic are available. For the latter, normalize Σᵢrᵢ = 1. All identities and support inequalities are homogeneous, so a rational solution can be scaled to an integer solution; conversely an integer solution normalizes to a rational one. This change of arithmetic preserves feasibility.

A solution groups equal Laurent exponents. The resulting homogeneous linear equations define a matching space on which the symplectic identity holds identically. An outside-space query searches for any remaining lift. A complete family classification requires this query to be UNSAT and every maximal space to equal the span of its time rescaling and species shifts. A separate mutation calculation must then certify finite type of its representative.

Verification and completeness

The alternating-chain identity

Suppose one sign vanishes on row i, and the other sign has exactly one monomial zᵃ in that row, in column j ≠ i. For a third row ℓ, put T = N+,ℓi(z⁻¹), U = N−,ℓi(z⁻¹), and V = N+,ℓj(z⁻¹), exchanging signs throughout when necessary. The (i,ℓ) identity is (1 + zᴿ)(T − U) = zᵃV, where R = rᵢ.

Assume T(1) = m + 1, U(1) = m, and V(1) = 2. Split all exponents into cosets modulo Rℤ. On each coset the coefficient recurrence is wₖ + wₖ₋₁ = vₖ. Finite support requires the two unit terms of V to lie on the same coset, at an odd distance. A single term of coefficient two cannot give finite support. Between the two unit terms, the coefficients of T − U alternate +1, −1, …, +1. Disjointness identifies these as the respective coefficients of T and U; their masses make the chain length 2m + 1.

In ascending order of the original polynomial exponents this gives pT,k = pT,0 + 2kR, pU,k = pT,0 + (2k + 1)R, pV,0 = pT,0 + a − R, and pV,1 = pT,m + a. These necessary linear relations settle rank-five constant pair 2067, where m = 8.

Inherited matching spaces and transport encoding

For a principal subdatum satisfying the exterior-column condition above, a match to a classified rank-three or rank-four constant pair also imports its complete matching-space equations. Relabeling the exponent variables and exchanging signs transports its saved rational row-reduced equations to the larger datum. The supporting lower-rank record and variable map can be reproduced with principal_relations.py.

An alternative exact multiset encoding uses nonnegative real transport variables tₑ𝒻 between formal exponent terms on the two sides. Their row and column sums are the respective coefficient multiplicities, and tₑ𝒻 > 0 requires e = f. A transport plan exists exactly when the evaluated exponent multisets agree: equal exponents may exchange their mass, and unequal exponents may not. Thus this encoding and the multiplicity encoding have identical feasibility.

Separating the connected slice loops

The cycle-length multiset of the terminal vertex permutation is preserved by relabeling, sign exchange, adjacent commuting mutations and cyclic rotation of the mutation loop. A cyclic rotation conjugates the terminal permutation. Two records with different multisets therefore cannot become equivalent under these moves.

For rank five, 42 records have unique cycle-length multisets. The other 13 records form five groups. Within each group the program exhausts cyclic rotations and commuting moves, including both signs, and compares full canonical encodings. All minima differ. The record stores this separating invariant and the exhaustive minimum where needed; it does not assert a universal canonical normal form for the singleton groups.

The verifier independently checks the constant matrices, supplies rational positive vectors, checks the matching identities and family dimensions, recomputes both reddening certificates and the labelled period, and replays every exclusion or coverage query. Each saved SMT formula has a SHA-256 digest. Distinctness under slice changes is checked using canonical cyclic mutation-loop encodings.

All 4,697 rank-five exclusion or coverage queries have successful UNSAT replays. The enumeration and lift checks have no unresolved cases. The 55 surviving families have independent periodicity and slice-distinctness certificates. See the reproduction instructions and file inventory.
